AI Act basics

Does the EU AI Act apply to my organisation?

Often yes, but not in the same way for every organisation or every AI use case. The correct starting point is to identify your role and assess each use case separately.

Last reviewed: 27 July 2026 · General information, not legal advice

The direct answer

An organisation that uses an AI system in its work may be a deployer under the AI Act. The obligations depend on what the system does, how it is used, who is affected and whether a specific rule applies. A company is not automatically “high-risk” as a whole.

A practical four-step check

1. Identify your role

Are you using an existing tool, providing an AI system, importing it or distributing it? Most ordinary organisations using third-party tools will mainly examine their position as deployers.

2. Create an AI inventory

List the systems, owners, departments, purposes, data and people affected. Do not start with legal labels before the use cases are clear.

3. Assess each use case

Recruitment, employee management, access to essential services, biometric uses, public-facing chatbots and generative content can raise different questions.

4. Match the relevant obligations

Consider AI literacy, prohibited practices, possible high-risk classification, transparency and any sector-specific rules.

Rules that may be relevant

Article 4 — AI literacy

Providers and deployers must take measures, to their best extent, to ensure a sufficient level of AI literacy for relevant staff and other persons using AI on their behalf.

Article 5 — prohibited practices

Certain AI practices are prohibited. Organisations should check selected use cases before deployment.

Article 6 and Annex III — high-risk AI

High-risk classification depends on the system, intended purpose and use. Recruitment and worker-management applications can require particular attention.

Article 50 — transparency

Specific transparency duties may apply to certain AI interactions, emotion recognition, biometric categorisation, deepfakes and AI-generated or manipulated content.

Simple examples

AI helps rewrite an internal emailAI literacy, data handling and internal rules may matter; this alone does not make the company high-risk.
A tool ranks job applicantsA high-risk assessment may be necessary because the use relates to recruitment.
A public-facing chatbot answers customer questionsTransparency duties and human escalation may be relevant.
Marketing staff use a public chatbot with client dataConfidentiality, data protection, tool rules and AI literacy require attention.

Application dates differ

The AI Act applies in phases. Prohibited AI practices and AI-literacy obligations started applying on 2 February 2025. Other provisions apply from 2 August 2026 or later, depending on the obligation and system category. Always verify the current implementation timeline.

What to do first

Create an accurate inventory and prioritise use cases that affect people, decisions, sensitive data or external communications. Then determine which obligations are relevant and which measures are proportionate.
